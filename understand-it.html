The Problem

Water insecurity isn't the same as scarcity.

A household can live beside a working well and still be insecure. The water might be unpredictable, costly, or unsafe to drink or cook with, even when a source sits right nearby.

For decades, that experience went unmeasured. Governments and researchers counted pipes, wells, and rainfall. None of it captured what water insecurity actually felt like.

Unpredictable
Water that comes and goes with no warning, on no schedule anyone can plan around.
Costly
Money or hours spent every day just to get water that should already be there.
Unsafe
A source nearby doesn't mean water that's safe to drink or cook with.
The Measurement

A short set of questions. A score that reflects real life.

Developed by Dr. Sera Young and collaborators worldwide, the WISE Scales ask about worry, disrupted plans, missed hygiene, and unmet drinking needs.

The whole survey takes about three minutes. What comes out is a score, not just a description, one that can be tracked, compared, and acted on.

The Rigor

Tested until it held its meaning everywhere.

Every question was piloted, revised, and translated into more than 45 languages, then tested again across dozens of countries and cultures.

That process is why a WISE score means the same thing everywhere it's used. A result from one country and a result from another can sit on the same chart and mean the same thing.

Piloted
Tested item by item before anything was finalized.
Translated
Rebuilt in more than 45 languages, not just word for word.
Validated
Checked again in each new country and culture before being trusted.
